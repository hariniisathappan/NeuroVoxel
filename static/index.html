QUANTITATIVE NEUROIMAGING WORKSPACE

See the signal inside the voxel.

NeuroVoxel is a browser-based research workspace for reproducible, region-of-interest radiomics on the Medical Segmentation Decathlon brain-tumour MRI set. Filter cases, calculate PyRadiomics features on demand, and compare sequences, sub-regions, patients and cohorts.

STEP 1 · ONE-TIME

Index the dataset

Reads each case once (volumes of the three tumour sub-regions, dominant sub-region, approximate hemisphere, quick consistency checks). Nothing is copied: your dataset folder is only read. Takes a few minutes for all 484 cases and is not repeated unless you ask.

HOW FEATURES ARE CALCULATED

Same settings for every case

    What this tool is, and is not. A measurement and exploration tool for open research data. Radiomic features are image-derived numbers, not diagnoses or predictions. The dataset has no age, survival or molecular information, so groups are defined by tumour size, sub-region and location only. Dataset: Medical Segmentation Decathlon, Task01 BrainTumour (BraTS-derived), CC-BY-SA 4.0 — Antonelli et al., Nat Commun 2022.

    HOUSEKEEPING

    Data & clean-up.

    Everything NeuroVoxel stores is re-creatable from your dataset folder. Your dataset itself is never modified.

    Clean up

    Temporary case files are the split sequences used by the viewer; they are re-created automatically when needed. Deleting calculated results keeps the dataset index, so you can calculate again without re-indexing. To start completely fresh, stop the app and delete the Docker volume (see README).

    Reading the numbers

    • Each sequence of each patient is z-scored on its brain voxels, then discretised with a fixed bin width of 0.25. That is why sequences are comparable.
    • Shape features depend on the mask only, so they are identical across sequences and are computed once per region.
    • Texture features are correlated with region size. The cohort page reports that correlation next to every effect size.
    • The segmentations were produced semi-automatically and reviewed; features partly reflect how a region was defined.
    • "Better" on this site only ever means "separates the groups more", never "more clinically useful".
